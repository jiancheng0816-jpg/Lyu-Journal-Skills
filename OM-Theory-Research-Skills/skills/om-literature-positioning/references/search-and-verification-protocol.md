# Search and verification protocol

## 1. Build the mechanism signature

Record:

- institution or market;
- focal actors and their objectives;
- timing and commitment structure;
- private and public information;
- information availability, acquisition, state knowledge, communication, commitment, experiment set, disclosure, and receiver response when relevant;
- endogenous decisions;
- decision ownership, policy adoption, policy intensity, and instrument set when relevant;
- operational or informational friction;
- equilibrium concept;
- headline result and comparative static.

Use these elements to search for structural neighbors that may use different terminology.

## 2. Search in layers

Search at least these layers when the task is a full positioning exercise:

1. exact construct and application terms;
2. mechanism synonyms and adjacent labels;
3. canonical theory underlying the model;
4. the same decisions in a different institution;
5. the same institution with a different mechanism;
6. backward and forward citations of the strongest seed papers;
7. recent working papers and forthcoming articles.
8. models that nest, are nested by, or reallocate control over the focal decision.
9. models using the same information method with a different operational response or preference geometry, and models studying the same operation under forecasting, cheap talk, signaling, screening, or persuasion.

Record representative queries and the search date. Do not claim systematic-review coverage unless inclusion criteria, databases, screening, deduplication, and exclusions are documented.

## 3. Verify bibliographic identity

- Match title, authors, year, outlet, volume, issue, pages or article number, and DOI where available.
- Prefer DOI and publisher records over aggregator metadata when they disagree.
- Distinguish a working-paper version from a published version.
- Preserve separate records only when versions materially differ.
- Do not invent missing DOI, pages, issue numbers, abstracts, or citation keys.

## 4. Verify substantive claims

- Locate the model primitives, decisions, mechanism, main result, and limitation in the source.
- Capture a short paraphrase and precise section, proposition, page, table, or appendix locator when available.
- Record whether the full text, abstract, or metadata was inspected.
- Treat snippets as discovery aids, not final evidence.
- State “full text unavailable” when access prevents verification.
- For the closest papers, identify the binding constraint, marginal trade-off, or equilibrium comparison that supports the claimed mechanism.
- For any claimed generalization or special case, verify the exact restriction that recovers the closest model and result.

## 5. Control false novelty

- Search the exact claimed mechanism and at least two plausible synonyms.
- Search both the application and the underlying theory.
- Examine papers that appear to contradict the novelty claim.
- Test whether the proposed difference changes equilibrium behavior or only notation and setting.
- Replace universal claims with search-bounded wording when coverage is incomplete.
- Search for papers that produce the same observable decision through a different mechanism; a similar outcome can be the most important comparison.
- Search for functionally equivalent instruments and alternative allocations of decision rights, not only papers using the same instrument name.
- For information papers, search each stage separately: acquisition or testing, credibility or reporting, experiment or disclosure design, receiver action, and interaction with pricing or contracting.
- Do not infer that papers using entropy, precision, transparency, or full disclosure study the same information order or experiment set.

## 6. Preserve reproducibility

Report databases or plugins, search strings, dates, filters, screening counts when available, and reasons for excluding apparent nearest neighbors. Keep the user's Zotero item key distinct from the BibTeX citation key.
