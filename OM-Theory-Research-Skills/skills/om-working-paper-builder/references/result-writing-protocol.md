# Formal-result writing protocol

Use the following analytical sequence for each lemma, proposition, theorem, or corollary. The sequence is mandatory in substance, but the exposition should normally read as continuous scholarly prose rather than as repeated labeled blocks.

## 1. Statement

- State all required parameter conditions and the equilibrium object.
- Distinguish existence, characterization, uniqueness, ordering, and comparative statics.
- Use weak or strict inequalities exactly as proved.
- Do not embed intuition or managerial claims in the formal statement.

Use result labels by function: lemmas for benchmarks and components, propositions for headline equilibrium or policy results, and corollaries for direct implications and comparisons.

When a formal result is reproduced or modified from prior work, label its provenance as `NEW`, `ADAPTED`, `SPECIALIZED`, or `INHERITED` in the internal result ledger and attribute the source in the manuscript. State what changed before claiming a contribution.

## 2. Equilibrium description

Explain what the players choose in each relevant region. Translate thresholds into behavior without dropping cases.

Name economically distinct regimes and keep the names stable across the introduction, analysis, figures, and conclusion.

## 3. Benchmark contrast

State what differs from complete information, the mechanism-removed benchmark, or the preceding environment. Identify which decision is independently distorted and which changes mechanically because of a constraint or identity.

## 4. Intuition

Explain the local economic forces in plain language. Identify direct effects, strategic responses, and selection or regime-switching effects.

## 5. Mechanism

Show the causal chain internal to the model:

`primitive or constraint -> changed marginal incentive -> endogenous response -> equilibrium result`.

State which assumption activates the mechanism and which extension preserves, attenuates, reverses, or replaces it.

For comparative statics, separate within-regime effects from discrete regime switches and distinguish direct, strategic, and selection channels.

For instrument comparisons, distinguish the mathematical marginal trade-off from the economic sources of signaling, commitment, screening, or operational power. State which source dominates and why.

For information design, trace `state or prior -> induced posterior -> receiver action -> sender payoff`. Explain disclosure regimes through their action consequences and preference alignment, not only through signal probabilities or informativeness labels.

For interacting levers, specify whether substitution or complementarity is a within-regime marginal response, an informativeness change, a discrete policy switch, an action effect, or a global policy comparison.

## 6. Literature distinction when material

If the result resembles a closest paper, compare the active constraint or marginal trade-off. Explain why the shared outcome does not imply a shared mechanism.

If the conclusion is consistent with prior work, identify the common directional or regime result and then explain whether it comes from the same mechanism. If the conclusion is opposite, identify the exact primitive, outside option, decision right, fee constraint, or equilibrium channel that reverses the effect. A citation alone is not a comparison, and an application difference alone is not a mechanism explanation.

When the focal model nests the closest paper, show the parameter or policy restriction that recovers its result before explaining the new region. When it does not nest the paper, avoid `generalizes` or `subsumes`.

## 7. Objection and alternative interpretation when material

For a headline new instrument, policy, or mechanism, answer the strongest plausible equivalence objection. Use a difference in decision ownership, feasible set, binding constraint, equilibrium existence, distortion, or comparative static rather than rhetorical insistence.

## 8. Managerial implication

Name the decision maker, observable condition, recommended direction of action, and limitation. Do not claim welfare or profitability beyond the modeled objective.

Integrate the implication into the result-group synthesis when a proposition has no distinct actionable content; do not invent a separate recommendation merely to fill a template.

Before using `optimal`, `preferred`, `dominates`, or `win-win`, name the objective or selection criterion, all stakeholders included, and the parameter region.

## 9. Result-chain link and visual crosswalk

Do not leave a formal result as a self-contained island. Its surrounding discussion should state, when applicable:

- which preceding proposition or benchmark it holds fixed, extends, contrasts with, or traverses in reverse;
- whether it introduces a new mechanism, changes the active constraint, or only remaps an earlier policy through a different primitive;
- what unresolved question motivates the next result;
- which figure panel, boundary, slope, jump, plateau, or profit path represents the result.

For a sequence of paired regimes, create explicit pairings in the prose, such as fixed versus endogenous instruments, product A versus product B, or cost versus search. For figures, explain the analytical role twice without repetition: before the visual, identify what is being mapped and how the axes move primitives; after the visual, interpret the economic contrast or distributional consequence. Never treat the visual as proof.

## 10. Proof

- Derive candidates and compare boundaries.
- Check feasibility, second-order conditions, deviations, and threshold ordering.
- Cover equality cases and all parameter regions.
- Cite earlier lemmas only when their conditions match exactly.
- End with a clear statement of what has been established.
- If a result relies on numerical optimization, state the analytical components, regimes and boundaries compared, global-search logic, and demonstrated parameter scope.

Place the full proof in the appendix by default for a journal-style narrative, while retaining the binding equation, first-order trade-off, or boundary comparison needed to understand the mechanism in the main text. Follow the user's or outlet's proof-placement preference when specified.

## 11. Consistency check

Verify that the abstract, introduction, figure captions, discussion, and conclusion state no stronger result than the formal claim.

Also verify that proposition discussions, cross-result comparisons, and figure interpretations use the same parameter regions, thresholds, regime labels, and mechanism attribution. A result described as a reverse mapping, corollary, or institutional contrast in one place should not be promoted to an independent mechanism elsewhere.
