# Closest-paper, instrument, and welfare protocol

Use this protocol when the main hurdle is whether the paper differs meaningfully from a specific predecessor, whether an endogenous decision erases the contribution, or whether a policy improves stakeholder welfare.

## 1. Decompose the analytical architecture

Create three separate ledgers.

### Policy and decision-right ledger

| Object | Benchmark | Revised paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy adoption | absent, mandatory, or voluntary | who decides and when |
| Policy intensity | fixed or endogenous | platform-, firm-, or consumer-determined |
| Observability | what agents see | what constitutes a credible signal |
| Enforcement | what makes commitment binding | modeled or outside scope |

### Instrument ledger

| Instrument | Strategic choice or mechanical consequence? | Feasible under which policy? | What imitation constraint does it relax? | Signaling cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Outcome ledger

| Regime | Equilibrium type | Firm profit | Success or transaction measure | Consumer surplus | Total surplus if defined |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not call a policy-induced increase in a variable the same instrument as a deliberate distortion of that variable unless the feasible sets, incentives, and deviation logic coincide.

## 2. Define the comparison metric

An instrument can be more effective along different dimensions:

- it supports separation in a larger parameter region;
- it supports separation when the benchmark pools;
- it lowers the high type's signaling cost;
- it raises the selected type's equilibrium profit;
- it improves consumer surplus or another stakeholder outcome;
- it reduces the distortion relative to the full-information allocation.

State the metric before using `more effective`, `outperforms`, or `dominates`. One instrument may dominate on signaling cost and lose on consumer surplus.

## 3. Use exact benchmark tests

Prefer decisive tests over broad contextual comparisons:

1. **Recovery test:** impose the proposed restriction and verify that the benchmark feasible set, payoffs, beliefs, and equilibrium are recovered.
2. **Equal-primitive test:** set the types' relevant primitive equal when the closest paper then predicts pooling; test whether the new policy still permits separation.
3. **Matched-choice test:** compare outcomes holding the strategic choices and information structure as similar as possible.
4. **Cost-gap test:** compare the minimum distortion or signaling cost needed to deter imitation.
5. **Ownership test:** transfer control of the policy parameter and determine whether the mechanism, not only the algebra, changes.

A side-by-side table should report conditions, active instruments, equilibrium strategies, and stakeholder outcomes. Follow the table with the economic reason for each difference.

## 4. Separate `if` from `how`

When the broad intuition that a policy can signal quality already exists, do not claim discovery of the idea. Reframe the contribution around:

- how the policy changes the relative effectiveness of existing instruments;
- how mandatory and voluntary adoption differ;
- who should choose policy intensity;
- when the new instrument lowers signaling cost;
- when one funding or market regime reverses the policy ranking;
- how implementation conditions limit the recommendation.

Use contribution verbs that match the evidence: `characterize`, `identify the conditions`, `show how`, `compare`, or `establish a boundary`.

## 5. Distinguish identification from welfare

A separating equilibrium lets agents infer type; it does not by itself establish positive consumer surplus or efficiency.

Before a welfare claim:

1. state the full-information allocation and investment or participation rule;
2. define consumer surplus, firm profit, success or transaction volume, and total surplus if used;
3. identify whether low-type participation is efficient or merely informed;
4. compare the policy equilibrium with the correct benchmark;
5. report stakeholder conflicts explicitly.

Use the following vocabulary carefully:

- **Protects consumers from private information:** type is revealed or harmful imitation is deterred.
- **Raises consumer surplus:** the defined surplus is strictly higher than the named benchmark.
- **Win-win:** every named stakeholder is weakly better off and at least one is strictly better off in the stated region.
- **Efficient:** the allocation maximizes the defined total-surplus objective, not merely separation probability.

If a policy yields separation and higher firm profit but zero consumer surplus, say so. Do not turn identification into welfare through wording.

## 6. Translate the comparison into policy guidance

Build a conditional decision rule rather than one unconditional recommendation:

| Institutional condition | Recommended policy | Intensity owner | Active instrument | Primary objective | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Relevant conditions may include enforcement capacity, moral hazard, production uncertainty, funding regime, type-cost difference, or the policy-intensity region. Explain why the recommendation changes across conditions.

## 7. Manuscript alignment test

The closest-paper distinction must appear consistently in:

- the abstract's result language;
- a dedicated introduction contribution paragraph;
- the closest-stream literature paragraph;
- the model's instrument and decision-right definitions;
- the proposition discussion containing the decisive comparison;
- the managerial implication and conclusion.

If the distinction exists only in the response letter or a comparison table, the reviewer cannot credit the paper for it.
